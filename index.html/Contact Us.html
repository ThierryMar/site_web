About the Instructor

Richard L. Lachance, Ph.D.

System Engineer | Astrodynamics Specialist | Space Sciences Instructor @ SpaceOrbitLAB.com

Dr. Lachance brings more than three decades of experience in space systems engineering, Earth remote sensing, optical instrumentation, numerical modeling, and mission-oriented space science. His teaching connects the mathematical foundations of orbital mechanics with the engineering realities of modern satellite missions.

At SpaceOrbitLAB, his objective is simple: help engineers, analysts, researchers, and advanced students understand spaceflight physics deeply enough to compute it, simulate it, and apply it to real mission problems.

Scientist. Engineer. Educator. Consultant.

Dr. Lachance combines academic teaching, industrial space engineering, applied research, and operational mission experience. His career has placed him at the intersection of:

  • Astrodynamics and orbital mechanics
  • Earth remote sensing and optical instrumentation
  • Numerical physics and scientific programming
  • Mission design and systems engineering
  • Space Situational Awareness and operational space applications

This combination gives students a practical perspective: the course is not limited to textbook orbit theory, but connects equations, algorithms, simulations, and mission constraints.

Featured Simulation

Discover Orbits101

Orbits101 is an interactive orbital simulator developed by Richard L. Lachance. It allows users to explore and visualize satellite trajectories by modifying their orbital parameters.

01

Explore Orbital Parameters

Orbits101 allows users to modify the classical orbital elements used to define the shape, size, and orientation of an orbit.

  • Semi-major axis (a)
  • Eccentricity (e)
  • Inclination (i)
  • Argument of periapsis (ω)
  • Right ascension of the ascending node (Ω)
  • Additional orbital parameters
02

Visualize the Trajectory

The simulated trajectory can be displayed in several commonly used coordinate systems, allowing users to compare how the same orbit is represented in different reference frames.

  • Earth-Centered Inertial (ECI)
  • Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed (ECEF)
  • True Equator, Mean Equinox (TEME)
  • Presets for well-known satellites
