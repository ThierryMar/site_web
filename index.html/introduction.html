Couse Overview

The program is divided into four courses. Students will begin with the basics of astronomy and celestial mechanics before exploring the foundations of astrodynamics, applied astrodynamics and Space Situational Awareness (SSA), and remote sensing from space.

You will learn to...

  • Work with real space data, including TLEs, tracking catalogs, conjunction data, and ephemerides.
  • Build scientific tools in MATLAB/Octave and Python, turning equations into working simulations and algorithms.
  • Understand Space Situational Awareness, including satellite tracking, space debris, conjunction assessment, and collision probability.
  • Connect orbits to remote sensing, from radiometry and atmospheric effects to optics, detectors, noise, and image formation.
  • Think like a mission analyst, validating models, assessing uncertainty, and translating physical results into operational decisions.

FROM EQUATIONS TO SPACE OPERATIONS

Modern astrodynamics goes beyond understanding equations. It requires knowing how to transform physical principles into models, simulations, and ultimately operational decisions.

Throughout the program, you will move progressively from fundamental physics to practical implementation. Concepts are derived from first principles, implemented through numerical simulations in MATLAB/Octave and Python, and applied to realistic mission scenarios.

You will learn not only why spacecraft move the way they do, but also how that knowledge is used in practice for orbit propagation, mission design, satellite tracking, conjunction assessment, collision avoidance, and Space Situational Awareness.

The objective is simple: turn mathematical theory into tools you can use to analyze and support real space missions.

BY THE END OF THE COURSE, YOU WILL BE ABLE TO:

  • Model and propagate spacecraft trajectories using analytical and numerical methods.
  • Analyze the perturbations that affect spacecraft in real operational environments.
  • Design orbital maneuvers, transfers, and mission trajectories.
  • Perform conjunction assessments and evaluate collision risks between space objects.
  • Model the space debris environment and understand its impact on space operations.
  • Propagate uncertainties and apply covariance analysis to orbital predictions.
  • Implement astrodynamics methods and simulations in MATLAB/Octave and Python.
  • Turn physical models and mathematical results into informed engineering decisions for real space missions.

WHO SHOULD ATTEND?

  • Aerospace engineers and scientists looking to strengthen their knowledge of astrodynamics.
  • Mission analysts and flight dynamics specialists working with spacecraft trajectories and operations.
  • Space Situational Awareness and Space Domain Awareness professionals.
  • Satellite operators seeking a deeper understanding of the physics behind orbital operations.
  • Graduate students and researchers working in space science, engineering, or related fields.
  • Professionals transitioning into the space sector who want a rigorous and practical foundation in astrodynamics.

WHY THIS COURSE IS DIFFERENT

This course goes beyond learning equations or using existing software tools. It is designed to connect the physical and mathematical foundations of astrodynamics directly to the way they are applied in modern space operations.

Major concepts are derived from first principles, implemented in MATLAB/Octave and Python, tested through numerical simulations, and validated against realistic scenarios and real-world data.

Rather than treating algorithms as black boxes, you will understand the physics behind them, build your own computational tools, and learn how to evaluate and validate their results.

The result is a learning experience that combines the rigor of academic astrodynamics with the practical methods used in mission analysis, spacecraft operations, and Space Situational Awareness.

WHY LEARNING ASTRODYNAMICS FROM PROFESSIONAL SOFTWARE IS SO DIFFICULT

Professional astrodynamics software is extremely powerful, but it is designed primarily to solve operational problems—not to teach the physics behind them.

Complex calculations are often performed behind the interface, making it difficult to see how orbital mechanics, perturbation models, reference frames, numerical methods, and estimation techniques actually work together.

Without this foundation, it can be challenging to understand why a model was selected, recognize when an assumption is no longer valid, or determine whether a result is physically meaningful.

This program takes the opposite approach: understand the physics first, implement the methods yourself, and then use professional software with greater confidence, technical judgment, and understanding.

"A PICTURE IS WORTH A THOUSAND WORDS"

Orbital mechanics becomes much easier to understand when equations can be seen in action. Interactive simulations transform abstract mathematical concepts into trajectories, geometries, and motions that can be explored visually.

By changing orbital parameters and immediately observing their effects, you can develop an intuitive understanding of concepts that are often difficult to grasp from equations alone.

Throughout the program, visualization and simulation are used alongside theory to connect mathematical models with the physical behavior of real orbital systems.
