Free-Body Dynamics

Free-body dynamics is a fundamental part of orbital mechanics. It describes how the motion of an object changes when one or more forces act upon it.

In a simplified orbital problem, gravitation is generally considered the main force acting on the object. Other effects, such as atmospheric drag, solar radiation pressure, and gravitational disturbances, may also be considered in more advanced models.

Key Principle

According to Newton’s second law, the acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and on its mass.

The Role of Gravitation

Gravitation is the attractive force that acts between objects with mass. In the case of a satellite, Earth’s gravitational attraction continuously changes the direction of its motion.

A satellite is therefore constantly falling toward Earth. However, its horizontal velocity allows it to move forward quickly enough that it continuously follows the curvature of the planet.

Key Takeaway

A satellite remains in orbit because its horizontal velocity and gravitational acceleration combine to produce a curved trajectory around a celestial body.

Practical Applications

Orbital mechanics plays an important role in the planning and operation of space missions. It is used to place satellites into orbit, calculate transfers between different orbits, and predict the future position of a spacecraft.

These calculations are essential for many modern technologies, including satellite communications, navigation systems, weather observation, Earth imaging, and scientific exploration.
