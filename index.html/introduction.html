Course Overview

This course bridges theory and practice in astrodynamics. You will learn how to model, simulate and analyze the motion of spacecraft and satellites in realistic environments, account for uncertainties, assess conjunction risks and apply these skills to mission design and operations. Through interactive lectures, hands-on computations and case studies, you will develop the analytical and programming skills required to tackle real challenges in spaceflight, navigation and space domain awareness.

You will learn to...

  • Propagate orbits using analytical and numerical methods
  • Model perturbations and assess their impact
  • Analyze relative motion and rendezvous dynamics
  • Propagate covariance and compute collision probability
  • Work with real catalogs of space debris and background stars
  • Apply astrodynamics to remote sensing mission analysis

Course highlights

FROM EQUATIONS TO SPACE OPERATIONS

Modern space missions depend on professionals who can transform the mathematics of celestial mechanics into operational decisions. From orbit determination and mission design to conjunction assessment, collision avoidance, and Space Situational Awareness (SSA) / Space Domain Awareness (SDA), the demand for engineers and scientists trained in advanced astrodynamics has never been greater.

This professional training program bridges the gap between theory and practice by combining rigorous physical foundations with hands-on computational implementation using MATLAB/Octave, and Python. Participants learn not only the “why” behind orbital dynamics, but also the “how” used daily by flight dynamics engineers, mission analysts, SSA specialists, and spacecraft operators supporting real missions.

Designed for aerospace engineers, researchers, graduate students, and space professionals, the course progresses from classical orbital mechanics to contemporary topics including perturbation theory, numerical orbit propagation, orbit determination, relative motion, conjunction analysis, collision probability estimation, space debris modeling, and remote sensing mission applications.

Unlike traditional courses that stop at deriving equations, this program emphasizes implementation. Every major concept is reinforced through realistic case studies, numerical simulations, and scientific programming exercises. Participants build working computational tools while developing the physical intuition needed to understand and validate their results.

This program is created to complement—not replace—professional astrodynamics platforms such as Systems Tool Kit (STK). Participants will develop a deep understanding of the underlying orbital mechanics, perturbation models, estimation techniques, and numerical algorithms that power these tools, enabling them to use commercial software with greater confidence, rigor, and technical judgment.

BY THE END OF THE COURSE, YOU WILL BE ABLE TO:

WHY LEARNING ASTRODYNAMICS FROM PROFESSIONAL SOFTWARE IS SO DIFFICULT

Professional astrodynamics software is extremely powerful, but it is designed primarily to solve operational problems—not to teach the physics behind them.

Complex calculations are often performed behind the interface, making it difficult to see how orbital mechanics, perturbation models, reference frames, numerical methods, and estimation techniques actually work together.

Without this foundation, it can be challenging to understand why a model was selected, recognize when an assumption is no longer valid, or determine whether a result is physically meaningful.

This program takes the opposite approach: understand the physics first, implement the methods yourself, and then use professional software with greater confidence, technical judgment, and understanding.

"A PICTURE IS WORTH A THOUSAND WORDS"

Orbital mechanics becomes much easier to understand when equations can be seen in action. Interactive simulations transform abstract mathematical concepts into trajectories, geometries, and motions that can be explored visually.

By changing orbital parameters and immediately observing their effects, you can develop an intuitive understanding of concepts that are often difficult to grasp from equations alone.

Throughout the program, visualization and simulation are used alongside theory to connect mathematical models with the physical behavior of real orbital systems.
